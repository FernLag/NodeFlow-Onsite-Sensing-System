How it works

  1. 1

    Describe one sensor per block

    Choose the sensor, the port it is wired into (A1 to A5, since A0 is taken by the shield's buttons), the measurement you want, and how it should look on the display.

  2. 2

    Fill in the numbers for your field

    Calibration values, soil texture, sensor depths. Each field carries the range it accepts and a note explaining where the number comes from. Defaults are filled in, but a default is a guess about your soil, not a measurement of it.

  3. 3

    Download and upload

    Press generate and the finished .ino lands in your downloads folder. Open it in the Arduino IDE, pick your Uno, press upload. The readings appear on the shield straight away.

What it supports

The generated sketch targets an Arduino Uno R3 with a 16×2 LCD keypad shield. The buttons sit on a resistor ladder on A0, so A0 is reserved and sensors use A1 to A5.

Irrometer Watermark 200SS

Soil water tension through a 200SS-VA3 adapter. Tension in kPa, a wet-to-dry percentage, or three management states derived from thresholds for your soil texture. The adapter carries up to three Watermarks and one shared temperature probe, outputs 0 to 2.8 V, and refreshes each channel every five minutes.

Irrometer 200TS temperature

Soil temperature, either on its own or paired with a Watermark on the same adapter. Reported in degrees Fahrenheit from the adapter's voltage curve.

DFRobot capacitive probe

A low-cost moisture probe wired straight to an analog pin. Raw counts, or a percentage once you have given it an air reading and a water reading.

Percentages always read higher when the soil is wetter. The one exception is the raw reading, which reports whatever the sensor actually put on the pin.

This page generates the code the NodeFlow(On-site) Sensing System runs to read the sensors you plug into it and produce the information you care about.

Full instructions are in the NodeFlow(On-site) guidelines and videos (links to be added).

To generate your code, you will need to know:

  • What sensors you want to connect
  • Which Arduino port each sensor goes into. Only one sensor per port.
  • For each sensor, what should appear on the LCD screen
  • How each of those values should be displayed

Working offline? This tool runs without an internet connection, but you have to open it online at least once so it can load. After that you can fill in the form and download your code with no connection. Any research information is sent the next time you are online.

Clearing your browser's cache or site data for this page removes the offline copy along with your saved details and anything still waiting to be sent. Open the page again while online to restore it.
